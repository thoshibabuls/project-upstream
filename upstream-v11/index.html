The problem

Your firm already knows more than it can easily find.

Mandates live in spreadsheets, contacts in inboxes and follow-ups in someone’s calendar. On a live mandate, that split shows up in five ways.

  1. 01 Duplicate outreach

    Two people email the same CFO.

    Each mandate keeps its own list, and nothing checks across them. Each email makes sense on its own. Together, they make the firm look disconnected.

    You find out from the CFO.

  2. 02 The unseen history

    Someone at the firm already spoke to them.

    A colleague approached the company on last year’s mandate. The history exists, but the person about to reach out cannot see it.

    You never find out.

  3. 03 The missed follow-up

    The follow-up date passes unnoticed.

    Nobody had the schedule open. By the time someone does, the thread has been quiet for three weeks, and reopening it costs goodwill you were saving for the right moment.

    You find out three weeks late.

  4. 04 The rebuilt list

    Every mandate rebuilds the same list.

    The sector map, buyer universe and contact research are rebuilt from scratch, even when the firm did the work last year.

    You pay for the same research twice.

  5. 05 The handover

    An analyst leaves.

    Their spreadsheets, their follow-up dates and the reason a buyer took the last call leave with them.

    You find out on the next mandate.

These look like five problems. They share one cause: the work is spread across files nobody else can see.
